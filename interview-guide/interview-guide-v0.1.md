# Interview Guide v0.1 - Semi-Structured Interviews

Study: Trust, Explainability, and Adoption of Generative AI Systems in Healthcare Organisations
Phase: 3 (explanatory, follows survey analysis)
Participants: clinicians, clinical safety/governance leads, digital and IT leaders
Duration: 45-60 minutes. Format: one-to-one, recorded with consent, transcribed for reflexive thematic analysis.

Status: working draft. Requires supervisor review and ethics approval. Questions will be refined after Phase 2 results to probe the strongest and most surprising quantitative findings.

---

## Opening (5 min)

Consent confirmation, recording consent, role and background warm-up:
*"Tell me briefly about your role and where, if anywhere, AI tools currently touch your day-to-day work."*

## Core questions

**Q1. Current exposure.** Walk me through the last time you used, or saw someone use, a generative AI tool (such as a chatbot or AI writing assistant) for a work task. What was the task, and what happened?
*Probes: sanctioned or informal? Outcome? Would you do it again? (RQ1, RQ3b)*

**Q2. Suitability boundaries.** Which parts of your work do you think these tools are suited to, and where would you never use them?
*Probes: what puts a task on the "never" side - risk, accountability, professional identity? (RQ1)*

**Q3. Organisational stance.** How would you describe your organisation's current position on generative AI - encouraged, tolerated, restricted, or silent? How did that position come about?
*Probes: who decided, what drove it, how is it communicated and enforced. (RQ3a)*

**Q4. Informal use.** Some staff in many organisations use public AI tools for work tasks without formal approval. How does that play out where you work?
*Probes: normalised or hidden? What does leadership know? Note: phrased in the third person deliberately to reduce self-incrimination pressure. (RQ3b)*

**Q5. Trust formation.** When you first used one of these tools, what made you more or less willing to rely on its output?
*Probes: fluency, speed, specific correct/incorrect answers, who recommended it. (RQ1a, RQ1c)*

**Q6. Trust after error.** Have you experienced one of these tools confidently producing something wrong? Tell me about it - what did you do next, and how did it change your use?
*Probes: graded adjustment vs abandonment; task-specific vs general loss of trust. (RQ1b, H1b)*

**Q7. Calibration.** How do you decide, in the moment, whether to accept an AI-generated output or verify it? What does verification actually look like in your workflow?
*Probes: time pressure effects; difference between clinical and administrative content. (RQ1)*

**Q8. Explainability.** Imagine two versions of the same tool: one cites the sources behind each answer so you can check them; the other explains its reasoning in fluent prose but cites nothing. How would each affect your willingness to rely on it?
*Probes: is a fluent explanation reassuring, and should it be? Has an explanation ever convinced you of something that turned out wrong? (RQ2a, H2c)*

**Q9. Conditions for organisational adoption.** If your organisation were deciding whether to formally adopt a generative AI tool for [participant's relevant task], what would need to be true for that to be done well?
*Probes: governance, training, clinical safety case, vendor assurances, staff involvement in rollout. (RQ3a, H3b)*

**Q10. Future stance.** Looking two to three years ahead, what is your honest expectation for how these tools will sit in your profession, and what worries you most and least about that?
*Probes: deskilling, accountability, workforce, patient relationship. (closing/synthesis)*

## Closing (5 min)

Anything important we have not covered; snowball request for further participants; thanks; explanation of next steps and findings sharing.

---

## Interviewer notes

- Do not correct participants' technical misconceptions during the interview; misconceptions are data.
- Distinguish consistently in notes between *predictive* AI experiences (risk scores, imaging) and *generative* experiences; participants conflate them.
- If a participant discloses unsanctioned use involving patient data, follow the ethics protocol on confidentiality boundaries (to be defined in `ethics/` before fieldwork - this is a live design question for the ethics application, not an afterthought).
