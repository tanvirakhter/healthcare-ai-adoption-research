# Paper Summary 02

**Asan, O., Bayrak, A. E., & Choudhury, A. (2020). Artificial Intelligence and Human Trust in Healthcare: Focus on Clinicians. *Journal of Medical Internet Research*, 22(6), e15154.**

## Why this paper

The most-cited framing of clinician trust in healthcare AI. Defines the trust problem this thesis extends to generative systems.

## Summary

A viewpoint paper analysing the determinants of clinician trust in AI and arguing that the goal is not maximum trust but *calibrated* trust: clinicians should trust the system exactly as much as its actual reliability warrants. Both over-trust (automation complacency, accepting wrong outputs) and under-trust (disuse, ignoring correct outputs) cause patient harm. The authors identify key trust factors: transparency and explainability of the model, perceived and actual accuracy, data quality and provenance, alignment with clinical workflow, and the clinician's own AI literacy. They also discuss liability ambiguity and the "black box" problem as structural barriers, and argue trust is dynamic, i.e. continuously updated through use rather than fixed at first exposure.

## Key concepts

- **Trust calibration** - matching trust level to actual system reliability
- **Over-trust vs under-trust** - both are failure modes with clinical consequences
- **Dynamic trust** - trust evolves through repeated interaction, errors, and recoveries
- Transparency, accuracy perception, workflow fit, and AI literacy as antecedents

## Relevance to this research

Trust calibration is arguably *more* difficult for generative AI than for the predictive systems Asan et al. discuss: fluent, confident language output gives stronger (and misleading) cues of competence than a probability score does, raising over-trust risk; publicised hallucination cases simultaneously drive under-trust. RQ1 directly extends this paper's framework. The dynamic-trust claim motivates including trust-erosion items in the survey (Q9-Q11) and an error-experience question in the interview guide (Q6).

## Critique / gaps

- Conceptual, not empirical; proposes determinants without measuring them.
- Predates generative AI; "explainability" is assumed to mean feature attribution for predictive models.
- Individual-level focus; organisational trust (governance, vendor relationships, institutional endorsement) is not addressed, which is the gap RQ3 targets.
