# Paper Summary 01

**Venkatesh, V., Morris, M. G., Davis, G. B., & Davis, F. D. (2003). User Acceptance of Information Technology: Toward a Unified View. *MIS Quarterly*, 27(3), 425-478.**

## Why this paper

The foundational acceptance model that most healthcare AI adoption studies build on, adapt, or argue against. Any extension of acceptance theory to generative AI has to position itself relative to UTAUT.

## Summary

Venkatesh et al. reviewed and empirically compared eight competing user acceptance models (including TAM, TPB, and the motivational model) using longitudinal data from four organisations, then unified them into UTAUT. The model proposes four direct determinants of behavioural intention and use: performance expectancy (will it help me do my job?), effort expectancy (is it easy to use?), social influence (do people who matter to me think I should use it?), and facilitating conditions (does the organisation support it?). Gender, age, experience, and voluntariness moderate these paths. UTAUT explained around 70% of variance in intention, substantially outperforming the individual models it integrated.

## Key constructs

- Performance expectancy (strongest predictor in most replications)
- Effort expectancy
- Social influence
- Facilitating conditions
- Moderators: gender, age, experience, voluntariness of use

## Relevance to this research

UTAUT supplies the structural backbone of the Phase 2 survey: four core constructs map directly to survey sections. But UTAUT contains no trust construct, no risk construct, and assumes the technology behaves deterministically. For generative AI in healthcare, all three omissions matter. The research strategy is to use UTAUT as the baseline and test whether generative-specific constructs (trust calibration, hallucination concern, explainability adequacy) add explanatory power beyond it.

## Critique / gaps

- Developed on workplace IT (e.g. accounting and scheduling systems), not safety-critical clinical tools.
- No account of trust erosion after errors, which is central to clinical AI.
- Voluntariness is binary in UTAUT; in healthcare, adoption is often organisationally mandated or organisationally prohibited, with informal "shadow use" in between, which UTAUT cannot represent.
