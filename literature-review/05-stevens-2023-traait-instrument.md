# Paper Summary 05

**Stevens, A. F., & Stetson, P. (2023). Theory of trust and acceptance of artificial intelligence technology (TrAAIT): An instrument to assess clinician trust and acceptance of artificial intelligence. *Journal of Biomedical Informatics*, 148, 104550.**

## Why this paper

The closest existing thing to the measurement instrument this thesis needs: a validated, clinician-centred model linking trust to AI acceptance, developed in the period when generative and ambient AI tools were entering real clinical settings.

## Summary

Stevens and Stetson develop and validate TrAAIT, a clinician-centred model and survey instrument, at an urban comprehensive cancer centre. The central hypothesis: system-specific trust in an AI tool is the primary predictor of acceptance (willingness to adopt) and of post-adoption general trusting stance towards AI. Trust itself is formed through three antecedents: information credibility, perceived application value, and reliability. The validated instrument is designed to be deployable by healthcare organisations to locate where adoption is breaking down (e.g. a credibility problem vs a workflow-value problem) rather than just whether clinicians "like" a tool. The authors also note that early hands-on exposure can build familiarity and ownership that raises adoption likelihood even when initial trust is imperfect.

## Key constructs

- System-specific **Trust in AI** as the central mediator
- Antecedents: **information credibility**, **perceived application value**, **reliability**
- Outcomes: **acceptance** (intention) and **trusting stance** (generalised attitude)
- Practical output: survey tool + summative score dashboard for implementers

## Relevance to this research

TrAAIT supplies validated item wording for the trust antecedents in the Phase 2 survey (Q5-Q11 draw on its credibility/value/reliability triad) and a defensible structural claim (trust mediates between system properties and intention) that the thesis can test in the generative context. Two extensions are proposed: (1) adding explainability-form variables as antecedents of credibility, testing the Ghassemi critique quantitatively; (2) testing whether the trust → acceptance path weakens when use is informal/unsanctioned, connecting to RQ3. Single-site validation at a US cancer centre also leaves an external-validity gap a UK multi-organisation sample can address.

## Critique / gaps

- Single institution, single country; generalisability untested.
- Treats "AI" largely as a unified category at the system level; does not isolate generative-specific failure modes (hallucination, prompt dependence).
- Organisational governance factors sit outside the model.
